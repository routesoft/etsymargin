Offsite Ads is the fee sellers most often get surprised by, because unlike the listing and
transaction fees, it doesn't apply to every sale — only specific ones, and the rules for which
ones aren't obvious from the seller dashboard alone.

## How it actually works

Etsy pays to advertise your listings on Google Shopping, Google Search, Facebook, Instagram,
Pinterest and Bing — you don't set a budget or choose which listings. If a shopper clicks one of
these ads and then buys **within 30 days** (even if they didn't buy on that visit), you're
charged an Offsite Ads fee on that order.

## The rate depends on your revenue

- **Under $10,000 USD in trailing 12-month sales**: 15% fee, and you can opt out entirely in
  shop settings.
- **$10,000 USD or more in trailing 12-month sales**: 12% fee, and participation becomes
  mandatory — you can't opt out once you cross the threshold.

The fee is capped at **$100 per order**, so on very large orders the effective percentage drops.

## Worked example

A $60 order that came from an Offsite Ad click, shop under the $10,000 threshold:

| Line | Amount |
|---|---|
| Order total | $60.00 |
| Standard Etsy fees (listing + transaction + processing) | ≈$5.15 |
| Offsite Ads fee (15%) | $9.00 |
| **Total Etsy fees** | **≈$14.15** |

That's roughly **23.6%** of the order — more than double the fee rate of a non-ad sale.

## Should you opt out, if you can?

If you're under the $10,000 threshold, opting out means you lose potential traffic from those
external placements, but you keep the full margin on any sale you'd have made anyway through
Etsy's own search. The honest trade-off: Offsite Ads work best for sellers with thin organic
reach who need the extra exposure and can absorb the fee on the incremental sales it brings.
If your margins are already tight (see our [margin benchmarks](/blog/good-etsy-profit-margin/)),
a 15% cut can turn a marginal sale into a loss.

## Once you're mandatory, price for it

Above $10,000 in trailing revenue, you can't opt out, so it's worth pricing with the 12% fee
built in as an assumed cost on a portion of your sales, rather than treating every Offsite Ads
charge as a surprise deduction from an otherwise-planned margin.
