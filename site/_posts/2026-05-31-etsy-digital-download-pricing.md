Digital downloads are the highest-margin product type on Etsy because there's no material cost
and no shipping cost — every sale after the first one costs you nothing extra to fulfill. That
also means Etsy's fees make up almost your entire cost side, so it's worth understanding them
precisely rather than approximately.

## The fee math is simpler here

With no shipping and no materials, the calculation drops two variables:

| Line | On a $8 digital download |
|---|---|
| Selling price | $8.00 |
| Listing fee | $0.20 |
| Transaction fee (6.5%) | $0.52 |
| Payment processing (3% + $0.25) | $0.49 |
| Total Etsy fees | $1.21 |
| **Net profit** | **$6.79** |
| **Margin** | **84.9%** |

That 85% margin is typical for digital products and is the main reason many sellers add
printables or templates alongside physical goods — they carry almost none of the operational
risk of physical fulfillment.

## Where the real cost is: your time, once

The genuine cost of a digital product isn't materials — it's the design and setup time before
the first sale. A planner template that takes 6 hours to design has a real cost of "6 hours of
your time," recouped gradually across however many copies sell. Price with that in mind: a
$5 printable needs roughly 50 sales just to be worth minimum-wage-equivalent for a 4-hour design
job, before it becomes pure profit.

## Common digital pricing mistakes

- **Pricing based on file size or "how much work it feels like," not perceived value** to the
  buyer. A one-page printable that solves a real problem can outsell an elaborate 40-page
  planner that doesn't.
- **Underpricing to compete with $1–2 listings** that are often low-quality or AI-generated in
  bulk. Competing on price in a race to the bottom rarely works for a single creator; competing
  on specificity and design quality usually does.
- **Forgetting bundle economics.** Bundling five $6 templates into a $20 bundle increases average
  order value and dilutes the fixed part of Etsy's fees (the $0.20 listing fee and $0.25
  processing flat fee) across a bigger sale.

## When digital margin isn't actually 85%

If you're using a paid tool to generate mockups, templates from a licensed asset pack, or
software subscriptions to produce the files, those recurring costs should be averaged across
expected monthly sales and subtracted before calling the margin "pure profit." A $20/month
design tool divided across 40 monthly sales is $0.50 of real cost per sale that a bare fee
calculation won't show.
