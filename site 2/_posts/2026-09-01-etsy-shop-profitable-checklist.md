Revenue and profit are easy to confuse when orders are coming in steadily. This checklist covers
the ten places a healthy-looking shop is most often quietly unprofitable.

## 1. Have you calculated margin including every Etsy fee?

Not just the 6.5% transaction fee — listing fee, payment processing, and offsite ads if
applicable. See the [full fee breakdown](/blog/etsy-fees-explained-2026/) if you haven't
itemized these recently.

## 2. Is your labor time actually costed in?

If "materials cost" doesn't include a reasonable hourly rate for your own time, your margin
number is measuring something other than whether the business works. Revisit our
[margin benchmark guide](/blog/good-etsy-profit-margin/).

## 3. Does your shipping charge cover the real shipping cost?

Carrier rate plus packaging materials plus Etsy's fee on the shipping charge itself — all three,
not just the postage sticker price. Detail in our
[shipping pricing guide](/blog/etsy-shipping-costs-without-losing-money/).

## 4. Have supplier or POD base costs changed since you last priced?

Material and print-on-demand base costs shift more often than most sellers recheck. A margin
that was fine six months ago may not be now.

## 5. Do you know your break-even point?

Fixed costs — subscriptions, Etsy Plus, workspace — need to be covered before any sale is real
profit. If you haven't calculated this, see our
[break-even guide](/blog/etsy-break-even-calculator-guide/).

## 6. Are your cheapest items disproportionately fee-heavy?

The fixed parts of Etsy's fees (listing fee, processing flat fee) hit low-priced items harder.
Check whether your $5–8 items are actually worth the shelf space compared to higher-priced ones.

## 7. Have you accounted for Offsite Ads on the sales it applies to?

If Offsite Ads are active on your shop, a portion of your sales carry a 12–15% fee that others
don't. Confirm your average margin reflects that mix, not just your best-case per-order number.

## 8. Are you tracking margin by product, not just shop-wide revenue?

A shop-wide revenue number can mask one or two products dragging down overall profitability
while others carry the business. Check margin per product line, not just total sales.

## 9. Does your pricing reflect current costs, or last year's?

Materials inflation, shipping rate increases and platform fee changes all compound quietly.
An annual price review, even a brief one, catches drift before it becomes a real problem.

## 10. Would you know if a price increase was needed?

If the answer to "should I raise prices" is a guess rather than a number, that's the gap this
checklist is meant to close. See our guide on
[raising prices without losing sales](/blog/how-to-raise-etsy-prices/) once you know you need to.

## If several of these are "no"

That's normal — most shops accumulate one or two of these gaps as they grow, not because of bad
decisions but because pricing usually gets set once and rarely revisited. None of them require a
big overhaul to fix; each one is a single number to recheck.
