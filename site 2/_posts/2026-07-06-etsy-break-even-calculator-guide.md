Per-order profit margin tells you whether a single sale is worth making. Break-even tells you
whether the *shop* is working — because fixed costs don't show up in a per-order calculation at
all, and they're what turns a "profitable" product into a shop that still loses money some
months.

## The formula

```
Break-even units = Monthly fixed costs ÷ Net profit per unit
```

If your net profit per item (after all Etsy fees, materials and shipping cost) is $12, and your
monthly fixed costs are $180, you need **15 sales a month** just to cover those fixed costs
before any of the 16th sale onward is genuine take-home profit.

## What counts as a fixed cost

- Etsy Plus subscription ($10/month), if you use it
- Photography, mockup, or design software subscriptions
- Paid keyword research tools (eRank, eRank Pro, similar)
- A dedicated workspace, storage unit, or studio rent allocated to the shop
- Business insurance or bookkeeping software

Materials, shipping cost, and Etsy's per-order fees are **not** fixed costs — they scale with
each sale and are already subtracted when you calculate net profit per unit.

## Worked example

A candle shop with:
- Net profit per unit: $9.40 (after Etsy fees, wax, wick, jars, shipping cost)
- Monthly fixed costs: $47 (a keyword tool subscription + Etsy Plus)

Break-even = 47 ÷ 9.40 = **5 units per month**. Every sale past the fifth is profit that isn't
absorbed by fixed overhead. For most small shops, this number is reassuringly low — the real
risk usually isn't hitting break-even, it's whether per-unit margin is thick enough to make
scaling past it worthwhile.

## Why this matters more than margin percentage alone

Two shops can have identical 35% margins and very different financial health if one has $20 in
monthly fixed costs and the other has $400. Margin percentage measures per-sale efficiency;
break-even measures how much volume you need before the business stops costing you money each
month.

## A caution on scaling fixed costs

Adding a new tool or subscription raises your break-even point immediately, even if it
eventually helps you sell more. Before adding a recurring cost, check how many extra sales it
needs to generate to pay for itself — a $30/month tool needs roughly three extra $10-profit
sales just to break even on its own cost, before it's netted you anything.
