This isn't really an "either/or" question for most sellers — it's about where each platform's
fee model actually helps you. The honest answer depends heavily on your sales volume.

## The core structural difference

- **Etsy** charges per sale: a $0.20 listing fee, 6.5% transaction fee, and payment processing —
  no fee at all if nothing sells. But Etsy also brings you buyers through its own search traffic,
  which Shopify doesn't provide on its own.
- **Shopify** charges a flat monthly subscription ($29–39+/month depending on plan) plus payment
  processing (roughly 2.9% + $0.30 with Shopify Payments), but has no per-listing or per-sale
  marketplace fee — and no built-in audience. You're responsible for all your own traffic.

## Where the crossover point sits

Roughly speaking, once a shop is doing enough monthly revenue that Etsy's 6.5% transaction fee
alone would exceed Shopify's flat subscription cost, Shopify starts looking cheaper on fees —
around $450–600/month in sales, depending on plan tier. Below that, Etsy's pay-only-when-you-sell
model usually wins on cost.

## Worked comparison: $2,000 in monthly sales

| | Etsy | Shopify (Basic + Shopify Payments) |
|---|---|---|
| Platform/subscription fee | $0 | ~$29–39/month |
| Transaction-style fees | ~6.5% + processing ≈ $210 | ~2.9% + $0.30/order ≈ $60–70 |
| Approx. total cost | ~$210–230 | ~$90–110 |

At this volume, Shopify's fees are meaningfully lower — but this comparison ignores the biggest
variable: **where the traffic comes from**. Etsy's fee effectively includes access to its search
and marketplace audience. A brand-new Shopify store with no existing following often sells
*nothing* without separately paid traffic (ads, influencers, SEO time), which can easily cost
more than the fee difference shown above.

## The realistic answer for most small sellers

Etsy tends to win for **discovery** — new sellers, low volume, or products people search for by
type rather than brand. Shopify tends to win for **retention and margin** once you have an
existing audience (social following, past customers, email list) that doesn't depend on Etsy
search to find you.

## A middle path

Many sellers who outgrow pure Etsy dependency run both: Etsy for new-customer discovery, Shopify
for a branded storefront that captures repeat buyers at a lower fee rate. The two aren't
mutually exclusive, and the fee comparison above is most useful for deciding where to route
*specific* products or customer segments, not an all-or-nothing platform choice.
