Etsy does have its own Profit Calculator — it's just tucked inside the listing editor, which
means most sellers only see it while actively creating a product, not while planning pricing
strategy beforehand.

## What Etsy's built-in calculator does

It lives inside the Pricing section of listing setup and lets you enter a price or profit goal
along with item costs, then shows the calculated retail price per variant. It's genuinely
accurate for the specific listing you're editing, since it's pulling live data from your own
shop settings.

## What it doesn't do

- **It doesn't work before you've started a listing.** You can't quickly test five pricing
  scenarios for a product you haven't built yet.
- **It doesn't project monthly profit** across expected sales volume.
- **It doesn't calculate break-even** against fixed costs like subscriptions or workspace rent.
- **It doesn't model country-specific extras** like the UK's regulatory operating fee or VAT on
  fees in a side-by-side, easy-to-compare way.
- **It doesn't let you save or compare scenarios** without switching between real listings.

## What a standalone calculator adds

A free tool like this one is built for the planning stage, before a listing exists: testing
whether a product idea is viable, comparing two price points side by side, projecting monthly
profit at different sales volumes, and calculating break-even against fixed shop costs — all
without touching your actual shop data.

## When to use which

| Situation | Best tool |
|---|---|
| Planning a new product before listing it | Standalone calculator |
| Fine-tuning the price of a listing you're actively creating | Etsy's built-in calculator |
| Comparing US vs UK vs EU fee impact | Standalone calculator |
| Checking break-even against monthly subscriptions | Standalone calculator |
| Confirming the exact number for a listing already in your shop | Etsy's built-in calculator |

Neither replaces the other — they're suited to different points in the pricing process.

## The one number that should always match

Whichever tool you use, both should agree closely on the core Etsy fees (listing, transaction,
processing) for the same price and cost inputs, since both are calculating against the same
published fee schedule. If they diverge significantly, double-check you're comparing the same
country's fee rates and the same offsite-ads assumption on both sides.
