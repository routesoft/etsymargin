Shipping is one of the easiest places for an Etsy shop to lose money without noticing, because
the mistake doesn't show up in any single number — it shows up as a margin that's consistently a
little thinner than it should be, across every order.

## The three shipping costs that aren't "the postage price"

1. **The actual carrier rate** — what USPS, Royal Mail, Canada Post, etc. charge you.
2. **Packaging materials** — box, mailer, tape, tissue paper, a thank-you card. Easy to forget
   because it doesn't feel like "shipping."
3. **The fee Etsy charges on shipping.** The 6.5% transaction fee and payment processing fee both
   apply to whatever you charge the buyer for shipping, not just the item price. Charging exact
   carrier cost as your "shipping fee" still means Etsy takes a cut of it — meaning strictly
   passing through your shipping cost without markup guarantees a small loss on every shipped
   order.

## Worked example: the shipping-fee gap

You charge $6 for shipping. Actual carrier cost is $5.80, and packaging costs $0.45. That's
$6.25 in real shipping-related costs against $6 charged — already $0.25 short before Etsy takes
its transaction and processing cut on that $6, which adds roughly another $0.55 (at 6.5% +
processing). Total shipping-related shortfall: about **$0.80 per order**, invisible unless you
add packaging cost and Etsy's fee on shipping into the calculation.

## How to price shipping correctly

- Add carrier rate + packaging materials cost, then round up slightly rather than down.
- Remember Etsy's fees apply to whatever you charge for shipping — either accept that small cut
  as a cost of doing business, or build a few percent buffer into the shipping charge to cover it.
- If you offer "free shipping," fold the real shipping cost (carrier + packaging + Etsy's fee on
  it) into the item price rather than absorbing it as a separate unaccounted loss.

## Free shipping and search ranking

Etsy's search algorithm has historically favored listings with free shipping over $35 orders in
the US, since it matches how the platform structures search filters for buyers. That can be a
legitimate reason to offer free shipping — but only if the item price already reflects the true
shipping cost. Free shipping funded by silently thinner margins isn't a pricing strategy, it's
an accounting gap.

## Multi-item orders change the math

Combined shipping for multiple items in one order usually costs less per item than shipping each
separately, but Etsy's transaction fee still applies to the full order total either way. Test
your actual combined-shipping settings against a couple of real order scenarios to confirm the
discount you're offering buyers isn't larger than the actual carrier savings.
