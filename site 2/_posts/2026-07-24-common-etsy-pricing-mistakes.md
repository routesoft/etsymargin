Most underpriced Etsy shops aren't pricing badly on purpose — they're making one of a small
handful of quiet miscalculations that compound over hundreds of sales.

## 1. Forgetting the transaction fee applies to shipping too

The 6.5% transaction fee and payment processing both apply to your **order total**, including
whatever you charge for shipping. Pricing shipping as a "pass-through" cost with no markup still
means Etsy takes its cut of it — that's not optional, so budget for it rather than being
surprised.

## 2. Copying a competitor's price without knowing their costs

A competitor selling below what looks sustainable for you might have a bulk materials discount,
a different (lower-cost) supplier, or simply be running at a loss without realizing it. Their
price tells you nothing about whether it's a profitable price — only your own cost structure
does.

## 3. Not costing in your own labor time

"Materials cost $4" isn't the same as "this product costs $4 to make" if it takes 45 minutes of
hands-on work. Fold a reasonable hourly rate into your cost calculation before judging whether a
margin is healthy — see our [margin benchmark guide](/blog/good-etsy-profit-margin/) for more.

## 4. Pricing every product the same regardless of order value

Etsy's fixed fees (the $0.20 listing fee, the flat part of payment processing) matter far more
on a $6 item than a $60 one. If your shop sells a mix of price points, the cheap items are
quietly carrying a much higher effective fee rate — sometimes worth bundling into multi-packs
to dilute the fixed cost.

## 5. Ignoring the Offsite Ads fee when it applies

A sale that looks like a solid 30% margin can drop to under 15% if it came through an Offsite Ad
click. If Offsite Ads are turned on (mandatory above $10,000 trailing revenue), build a buffer
into pricing rather than assuming every sale nets the full margin. Detail in our
[Offsite Ads guide](/blog/etsy-offsite-ads-fee-explained/).

## 6. Not re-checking prices after a POD or supplier cost increase

Print-on-demand base costs and raw material prices change more often than most sellers
re-check their listings. A margin that was healthy six months ago silently erodes if the
underlying cost went up and the price didn't follow.

## 7. Confusing markup with margin

A 100% markup on cost is only a 50% margin on price — not the same number, and mixing them up
consistently means underpricing. If you're mentally targeting "50% margin" but calculating
markup instead, you're leaving money on every sale.

## The fix for all seven

Every one of these mistakes comes from calculating price based on a partial picture — cost
without labor, price without shipping fees, margin without checking it's actually margin. The
fix is the same each time: run the complete number, not the convenient one.
